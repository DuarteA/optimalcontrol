Motivation

In policy gradient methods the gradient is only a local approximation of policy improvement. Two issues follow:

  • A large update can radically change the policy distribution.
  • The policy gradient objective does not guarantee performance improvement.

Trust region methods handle this by insisting that

  • policy improvement be local in distribution space, not in parameter space;
  • the update guarantee improvement, with the parameter step controlled by a line search.

TRPO is complex and has many hyperparameters. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a simplification of it with fewer.

TRPO

Central to TRPO is the following optimization problem, over a batch \((x_i,u_i)\), \(i\in\{1,\dots,M\}\) generated by \(\pi_{\text{old}}\):

\[\min_\theta L(\theta), \qquad L(\theta)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M\left[\frac{\pi_\theta(u_i\mid x_i)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(u_i\mid x_i)}A_{\pi_{\text{old}}}(x_i,u_i)\right]\]

\[\text{s.t.}\qquad \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M\big[D_{\text{KL}}\big(\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot\mid x_i)\,\|\,\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x_i)\big)\big]\le\delta\]

  • \(\theta\) – parameters of the new policy, to be optimized
  • \(\pi_\theta(u\mid x)\) – probability of action \(u\) given state \(x\) under the new policy; \(\pi_{\text{old}}(u\mid x)\) the same under the old policy
  • \(A_{\pi}(x,u)=Q_{\pi}(x,u)-J_{\pi}(x)\) – advantage function of the old policy
  • \(D_{\text{KL}}\) – Kullback-Leibler divergence between the old and the new policy

Intuitively: improve the advantage using the old policy’s data and an importance sampling ratio, subject to the new policy’s distribution staying not far from the old one – the trust region.

Convention: this course minimises a cost, so a good action has a negative advantage and \(L\) is minimised. The original papers maximise a reward and carry an extra minus sign.

TRPO

In practice the cost and the constraint are approximated linearly and quadratically, and the search direction for the new control parameters is

\[\theta_{k+1}=\theta_k-\alpha F^{-1}g\]

  • \(g\) – gradient of the cost

  • \(F\) – matrix to improve gradient directionality (the Fisher information matrix of the policy)

  • \(\alpha\) – picked by line search, to guarantee improvement; a step size, not the discount factor

  • The line search guarantees both that the new control parameters are better than the previous ones and that the constraint is met.

  • The trust region applies only to the policy, not to the value/advantage function (the critic).

  • For the value/advantage function, Monte-Carlo or semi-gradient TD can be used. The original TRPO paper uses a more advanced method.

TRPO

Algorithm: main ideas behind full TRPO

Initialize the policy parameters \(\theta\) and the value function parameters \(\phi\). Then repeat:

  1. Collect trajectories using \(\pi_\theta\).
  2. Estimate the advantages \(A_t\).
  3. Compute the policy gradient \(g\) and the matrix \(F\) (omitted here).
  4. Compute the search direction by conjugate gradient.
  5. Line search, to satisfy the KL constraint.
  6. Update the policy parameters, \(\theta\leftarrow\theta-\alpha F^{-1}g\).
  7. Update the value/advantage function parameters \(\phi\).

Because of step 1 this is an on-policy method, like SARSA.

Also because of step 1 it needs a full batch of data for a single policy update, which is quite inefficient.

PPO

TRPO is a rather complex algorithm, whose original form even carries guarantees of policy improvement. PPO is the simplification of it that is used in practice. It solves, over the same kind of batch generated by \(\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}\),

\[\min_\theta L(\theta), \qquad L(\theta)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M\big[\max\big(r_i(\theta)A_{\pi_{\text{old}}}(x_i,u_i),\;c_i(\theta)A_{\pi_{\text{old}}}(x_i,u_i)\big)\big]\]

\[r_i(\theta)=\frac{\pi_\theta(u_i\mid x_i)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(u_i\mid x_i)}, \qquad c_i(\theta)=\max\big(\min(r_i(\theta),1+\epsilon),1-\epsilon\big), \qquad A_{\pi}(x,u)=Q_{\pi}(x,u)-J_{\pi}(x)\]

\(c_i(\theta)\) is \(r_i(\theta)\) clipped to \([1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]\): a simple way of ensuring the policy does not change much, so the trust region is enforced implicitly rather than as a constraint.

As on the previous slide: minimising a cost takes the pessimistic branch, which here is the maximum. The reward-maximising form in the original paper takes the minimum of the same two terms.

TRPO vs PPO

Trust region

TRPO enforces a hard KL constraint in policy space. PPO has no constraint: the trust region is enforced implicitly by clipping, inside an unconstrained problem.

Optimization

TRPO is a second-order method – the matrix \(F\) – followed by a line search. PPO is ordinary stochastic gradient descent.

Use of a batch

TRPO performs one policy update per batch of data. PPO performs multiple epochs per batch.

In practice

TRPO is too complex and is rarely used. PPO is widely used, for instance in robotics.

Motivation

Standard actor-critic methods parameterize a stochastic policy, but the parameters they converge to usually make it almost deterministic – for instance the covariance of a Gaussian policy shrinks towards zero. This is exactly what the mountain car of Unit 13 did.

A way to capture this is the entropy of the policy. For a discrete action space,

\[H(x)=-\sum_u\pi(u\mid x)\log\big(\pi(u\mid x)\big).\]

Low entropy means the policy is close to deterministic (a Dirac); high entropy means close to uniform.

So: usual actor-critic methods typically converge to policies of low entropy. Low-entropy and deterministic policies do not explore the input space, and therefore often cannot find good policy improvements. Randomized tricks are usually bolted on instead – \(\epsilon\)-soft policies, for example.

Soft actor-critic

Add a reward for entropy to the cost function – that is, subtract the entropy from the cost being minimised:

\[\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha^k\Big(g(x_k,u_k)-\lambda H\big(\pi(\cdot\mid x_k)\big)\Big)\right], \qquad H\big(\pi(\cdot\mid x_k)\big)=-\mathbb{E}_u\big[\log\big(\pi(u\mid x_k)\big)\big]\]

\[\textbf{discrete input spaces}\qquad H=-\sum_u\pi(u\mid x_k)\log\big(\pi(u\mid x_k)\big)\]

\[\textbf{continuous input spaces}\qquad H=-\int_u\pi(u\mid x_k)\log\big(\pi(u\mid x_k)\big)\,du\]

The weighting factor \(\lambda\) is called the temperature: the higher the temperature, the more stochastic the policy is.

The SAC literature writes the temperature \(\alpha\) and the discount \(\gamma\); this course uses \(\alpha\) for the discount throughout, so the temperature is \(\lambda\) here. The literature also maximises a reward, where the entropy term is added rather than subtracted.

Soft actor-critic

Soft actor-critic has some other features, namely

  • Two critics, \(Q_{\phi_1}\) and \(Q_{\phi_2}\), with parameters \(\phi_1\) and \(\phi_2\).
  • A stochastic policy \(\pi_\theta(u\mid x)\) with parameters \(\theta\), typically Gaussian with learned mean and covariance.
  • It uses replay buffers, as deep Q-learning did in Unit 12.
  • It uses stochastic gradient for the critic update.

Train a soft actor-critic agent to control a robot arm for a ball-balancing task. Open here.

  • The control inputs to the manipulator are the torque signals for the actuated joints.
  • The simulation ends when the ball falls off the plate.
  • The reward is

\[r_t=r_{\text{ball}}+r_{\text{plate}}+r_{\text{action}}, \qquad r_{\text{ball}}=e^{-0.001(x^2+y^2)}, \qquad r_{\text{plate}}=-0.1\big(\phi^2+\theta^2+\psi^2\big), \qquad r_{\text{action}}=-0.05\big(\tau_1^2+\tau_2^2\big)\]

where \(x,y\) is the position of the ball in the plane, ideally zero; \(\phi,\theta,\psi\) are the end-effector roll, pitch and yaw; and \(\tau_1,\tau_2\) are the torques.

Concluding remarks

  • TRPO solves an optimization problem that enforces policy improvement for a given batch of data.
  • That problem is hard, and in practice it is replaced by the simpler one used by PPO.
  • The idea behind both is the same: ensure the learning policy does not take big steps away from the previous policy.
  • PPO is widely used in applications.
  • Soft actor-critic uses the policy’s entropy to promote exploration – the failure the mountain car of Unit 13 exhibited, named and priced into the objective.
  • This idea is used and combined in many other methods.
