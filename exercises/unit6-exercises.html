1 Core exercises

Problem 6.1 The goal of this exercise is to repeat Problem 5.1 but considering state disturbances and noisy measurements. To this effect consider a first order discrete-time linear system

\[x_{k+1} = \alpha x_k+u_k+w_k, \ \ k\in \mathbb{N}_0,\]

where \(\{w_k\}\) are Gaussian, independent and identically distributed, with variance \(\mathbb{E}[w_k^2]=\sigma_w^2\), and consider the cost

\[\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{k=0}^q x_k^2+\gamma u_k^2\Big].\]

  1. Considering \(\alpha=1\), \(\gamma=1\), \(q=2\), \(\sigma_w=1\), determine the policy for \(u_0,u_1,u_2\) that minimizes the cost when:    (i) the value of \(x_k\) is known at every stage \(k\).    (ii) \(x_k\) is measured at every stage by a noisy sensor \(y_k=x_k+n_k\), where \(\{n_k\}\) are Gaussian iid with variance \(\mathbb{E}[n_k^2]=\sigma_n^2\), \(\sigma_n=\frac{1}{3}\), and \(x_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,10)\).
  2. Considering \(\alpha=1\), \(\gamma=1\), and the averaged cost \(\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{1}{q+1}\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{k=0}^q x_k^2+\gamma u_k^2\big]\), determine the policy for \(\{u_k\}\) that minimizes the cost when:    (i) \(x_k\) is known at every stage.    (ii) \(x_k\) is measured as in (a)(ii), with the same distribution for \(x_0\).
NoteSolution

(a)(i) By certainty equivalence, the policy is the same as in Problem 5.1(a): \(u_2=\mu_2(x_2)=0\), \(u_1=\mu_1(x_1)=-\frac{1}{2}x_1\), \(u_0=\mu_0(x_0)=-\frac{3}{5}x_0\).

(a)(ii) Let \(I_0=\{y_0\}\), \(I_1=\{u_0,y_0,y_1\}\), \(I_2=\{u_0,y_0,y_1,u_1,y_2\}\). By the separation principle, the optimal policy replaces the state with its Kalman-filter estimate:

\[u_2=\mu_2(I_2)=0 \qquad u_1=\mu_1(I_1)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\hat x_{1|1} \qquad u_0=\mu_0(I_0)=-\tfrac{3}{5}\hat x_{0|0}\]

Since \(u_2=0\), we only need \(L_0\) and \(L_1\). With \(\hat x_{0|-1}=\bar x_0=0\), \(\Phi_{0|-1}=10\), \(A=B=C=1\), \(W=1\), \(V=\frac{1}{9}\), iterating \(\Phi_{k+1|k}=A\Phi_{k|k}A^\intercal+W\), \(\Phi_{k|k}=\Phi_{k|k-1}-\Phi_{k|k-1}C^\intercal(C\Phi_{k|k-1}C^\intercal+V)^{-1}C\Phi_{k|k-1}\), \(L_k=\Phi_{k|k-1}C^\intercal(C\Phi_{k|k-1}C^\intercal+V)^{-1}\):

\[\Phi_{0|0}=10-\frac{10^2}{10+\frac19}=\frac{10}{91}, \ \ L_0=\frac{10}{10+\frac19}=\frac{90}{91} \qquad \Phi_{1|0}=\frac{10}{91}+1=\frac{101}{91}, \ \ L_1=\frac{101/91}{101/91+1/9}=0.909\]

so \(\hat x_{0|0}=\frac{90}{91}y_0\), \(\hat x_{1|0}=\frac{90}{91}y_0+u_0\), \(\hat x_{1|1}=\hat x_{1|0}+0.909(y_1-\hat x_{1|0})\).

(b)(i) For the average-cost problem the policy coincides with the deterministic infinite-horizon policy from Problem 5.1(b): \(u_k=-0.618x_k\).

(b)(ii) By the separation principle, \(u_k=-0.618\hat x_{k|k}\), where \(\hat x_{k|k}\) is now the stationary Kalman filter estimate (appropriate for an average-cost criterion, where the initial transient is irrelevant): \(\hat x_{k+1|k}=\hat x_{k|k}+u_k\), \(\hat x_{k|k}=\hat x_{k|k-1}+L(y_k-\hat x_{k|k-1})\), \(L=\frac{\Phi}{\Phi+1/9}\), where \(\Phi\) is the positive solution of \(\Phi^2=\Phi+\frac19\), i.e. \(\Phi=1.1009\) (discarding the negative root \(-0.1009\)), giving the stationary gain \(L=0.9083\).

Problem 6.2 Consider the system

\[x_{k+1} = \frac56x_k+u_k+w_k\]

with open-loop policy \(u_k=1\) for every \(k\in\mathbb{N}_0\), and \(w_k\) zero-mean iid Gaussian with \(\mathbb{E}[w_k^2]=1\). We wish to estimate \(x_k\) based on the measurements

\[y_\ell = 2x_\ell+\tfrac12v_\ell, \ \ \ell\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}\]

where \(v_\ell\) are zero-mean iid Gaussian with \(\mathbb{E}[v_\ell^2]=2\), and \(x_0\sim\mathcal{N}(\frac12,1)\).

  1. Show that the Kalman filter estimate \(\hat x_{2|2}=\mathbb{E}[x_2|y_0,y_1,y_2,u_0{=}1,u_1{=}1]\) can be written as \(\hat x_{2|2}=a_0y_0+a_1y_1+a_2y_2+c\), and give \(a_0,a_1,a_2,c\).
  2. Show that the stationary estimate \(\hat x_{k|k}=\mathbb{E}[x_k|y_0,\dots,y_k,u_0{=}1,\dots,u_{k-1}{=}1]\) can be written as \(\hat x_{k|k}=\sum_{\ell=0}^k h_{k-\ell}y_\ell+d_k\), and give an expression for \(h_k\) and \(d_k\).
NoteSolution

Not reproduced here – taken from the TU/e 2015/2016 sample exam 1, whose full worked solution is available in the exam archive rather than in SolutionsPS2.tex. The approach follows the standard Kalman filter recursion (prediction with \(A=\frac56\), \(B=1\), \(W=1\); correction with \(C=2\), \(V=\frac12\)) applied twice by hand for (i), then generalized to the stationary (converged) gain for (ii), expressing each \(\hat x_{k|k}\) as a linear combination of past outputs – structurally the same computation as Problem 6.5, with a nontrivial \(A\) and \(C\).

Problem 6.3 Consider a mass-spring-damper system for which the mass displacement \(z(t)\) is described by

\[m\ddot z(t) = -c_f\dot z(t)-k_sz(t)+f_d(t), \ \ t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\]

where \(m=1\), \(k_s=5\), \(c_f=1\), and \(f_d(t)\) models force disturbances.

  1. Find a state-space representation considering \(x(t)=[z(t)\ \ \dot z(t)]^\intercal\).
  2. Find a discrete-time system describing \(x(k\tau)\) for \(\tau=0.1\), approximating \(f_d(t)\approx f_d(k\tau)\) where \(f_d(k\tau)\) are zero-mean iid Gaussian with \(\mathbb{E}[f_d(k\tau)^2]=0.1\).
  3. Suppose the output \(y(k\tau)=Cx(k\tau)+n_k\) is available, \(n_k\) zero-mean iid Gaussian with \(\mathbb{E}[n_k^2]=\sigma_n^2\).    (i) Provide the Kalman filter estimate of \(x(k\tau)\) for \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), assuming the initial state covariance is \(2I\).    (ii) Provide the stationary Kalman filter and the asymptotic error covariance matrix.
NoteSolution

A structurally identical problem (same physical setup, mass-spring system with position measurements) is worked out in full in the course slides – see Lecture 6, Appendix C (“Example: Kalman filter”, lectures/lecture06-kalman-lqg.qmd), which discretizes an analogous mass-spring system, sets up \(W\) and \(V\), and iterates the Kalman filter equations by hand for \(k=0,1,2\); follow the same steps here with \(m=1\), \(k_s=5\), \(c_f=1\) in place of the lecture’s parameters.

Problem 6.4 Consider the discrete-time linear system

\[x_{k+1} = Ax_k+Bu_k+\omega_k, \ \ k\in\mathbb{N}_0 \qquad A=\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac15\\0&1\end{bmatrix} \quad B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix} \quad x_0=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\]

  1. Suppose \(\omega_k=0\) for every \(k\). Compute the optimal \(u_0,u_1,u_2\) minimizing \(J=\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}x_k^\intercal Qx_k+u_k^\intercal Ru_k\) for \(h=3\), \(Q=I\), \(R=0.1\).
  2. Suppose \(\omega_k\) are zero-mean iid Gaussian with \(\mathbb{E}[\omega_k\omega_k^\intercal]=\frac12I\) and the full state is available. Compute the optimal policy \(u_k=\mu_k(x_k)\), \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), minimizing \(\mathbb{E}[J]\).
  3. Suppose \(\omega_k\) are as in (b) but only \(y_k=Cx_k+n_k\) is available, \(C=[1\ \ 0]\), \(n_k\) zero-mean iid Gaussian with \(\mathbb{E}[n_k^2]=\frac14\).    (i) Compute the optimal policy \(u_k=\mu_k(I_k)\), \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), minimizing \(\mathbb{E}[J]\).    (ii) Compute the optimal policy minimizing the average cost \(\lim_{h\to\infty}\frac1h\mathbb{E}[J]\).
  4. Suppose now the actuators can only be updated at even times \(k=2\ell\) (multi-rate control), \(u_k=0\) at odd \(k\).    (i) Compute the optimal policy \(u_k=\mu_k(I_k)\), \(k\in\{0,2\}\), minimizing the cost of (c)(i).    (ii) Compute the optimal policy for \(k=2\ell\) minimizing the average cost of (c)(ii).
NoteSolution

(a) This is a standard (deterministic) finite-horizon LQR problem; solving the Riccati recursion with \(P_3=0\) gives \(P_2=I\), \(P_1=\begin{bmatrix}2&0.2\\0.2&1.1309\end{bmatrix}\), \(K_2=0\), \(K_1=[0\ \ {-\frac1{1.1}}]\), \(K_0=[-0.1625\ \ {-0.9513}]\). Propagating \(x_0=[1\ \ 1]^\intercal\) through the dynamics with \(u_k=K_kx_k\) gives \(u_0=-1.1137\), \(x_1=[1.2\ \ {-0.1137}]^\intercal\), \(u_1=0.1034\), \(u_2=0\).

(b) By certainty equivalence, the optimal policy is identical to (a): the disturbances change the cost but not the optimal gains \(K_0,K_1,K_2\) (since \(x_0\) is deterministic, \(u_0\) is known a priori, but \(u_1\) depends on the realization of \(w_0\) through \(x_1\)).

(c)(i) By the separation principle, \(u_k=K_k\hat x_{k|k}\) with the same gains as (a)-(b), and \(\hat x_{k|k}\) the Kalman filter estimate (\(\hat x_{0|-1}=\bar x_0=[1\ \ 1]^\intercal\), \(\Phi_{0|-1}=0\) since \(x_0\) is known exactly, \(W=\frac12I\), \(V=\frac14\)). Iterating gives \(\Phi_{0|0}=0\), \(L_0=0\) (so \(\hat x_{0|0}=x_0\) regardless of \(y_0\)), \(\Phi_{1|0}=\frac12I\), \(L_1=\frac23[1\ \ 0]^\intercal\). Since \(K_2=0\) and \(K_1L_1=0\) (the second component of \(K_1\) multiplies a zero entry of \(L_1\)), it turns out \(u_0,u_1,u_2\) all end up deterministic – numerically identical to the noise-free path in (a): \(u_0=-1.1137\), \(u_1=0.1034\), \(u_2=0\). This is a special feature of this particular \(A,B,C\), not a general fact about output feedback.

(c)(ii) By the separation principle for the average-cost problem, \(u_k=K\hat x_{k|k}\) with \(K\) the infinite-horizon LQR gain and \(\hat x_{k|k}\) from the stationary Kalman filter. Solving the two algebraic Riccati equations (control and estimation) numerically gives \(P=\begin{bmatrix}6.6069&1.2044\\1.2044&1.3506\end{bmatrix}\), \(\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}0.8781&0.7510\\0.7510&3.4230\end{bmatrix}\), \(L=\begin{bmatrix}0.7784\\0.6658\end{bmatrix}\), \(K=[-0.8303\ \ {-1.0971}]\).

(d)(i)-(ii) Grouping two time-steps into one (\(\bar x_\ell:=x_{2\ell}\), \(\bar u_\ell:=u_{2\ell}\)) turns the multi-rate problem into a standard single-rate problem with \(\bar A=A^2\), \(\bar B=AB\), \(\bar Q=Q+A^\intercal QA\), \(\bar R=R+B^\intercal QB\), \(\bar S=A^\intercal QB\), and lumped disturbance \(\bar w_\ell=Aw_{2\ell}+w_{2\ell+1}\) (still Gaussian iid, since sums of Gaussians are Gaussian). For (d)(i) (finite horizon, \(h=3\)) this gives the single-decision gain \(\underline K_0=[-0.0935\ \ {-0.9720}]\) and, since \(\hat x_{0|0}=x_0\) exactly, \(u_0=-1.0654\) independent of \(y_0\). For (d)(ii) (average cost) the same reasoning with the algebraic Riccati/Kalman equations for \((\bar A,\bar B,\bar Q,\bar R,\bar S)\) gives \(\bar P=\begin{bmatrix}6.6274&1.2045\\1.2045&1.3536\end{bmatrix}\), the same \(\Phi\), \(L\) as (c)(ii), and gain \(\bar K=[-0.7905\ \ {-1.1269}]\).

Problem 6.5 Consider the problem of estimating the value of a constant \(x_0\) from noisy measurements

\[y_k = x_0+n_k, \ \ k\in\mathbb{N}_0,\]

where \(n_k\) are zero-mean iid Gaussian with variance \(\mathbb{E}[n_k^2]=\sigma_n^2\). The initial state \(x_0\) is also Gaussian with mean \(\bar x_0\) and variance \(\phi_0=\mathbb{E}[(x_0-\bar x_0)^2]\).

  1. Compute the Kalman filter estimate \(\hat x_k\) of \(x_0\) (the one minimizing the error variance \(\mathbb{E}[(x_k-\hat x_k)^2]\)) at times \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), based on \(I_0=\{y_0\}\), \(I_1=\{y_0,y_1\}\), \(I_2=\{y_0,y_1,y_2\}\), and the associated error variances. [Suggestion: note \(y_k=x_k+n_k\) for \(x_{k+1}=x_k\).]
  2. Compute the asymptotic error variance \(\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[(x_k-\hat x_k)^2|I_k]\) and explain the value obtained.
NoteSolution

(i) Since \(x_{k+1}=x_k\), \(y_k=x_k+n_k\), the disturbance covariance is \(W=0\). The standard Kalman filter equations reduce to \(\hat x_{k+1|k}=\hat x_{k|k}\), \(\hat x_{k|k}=\hat x_{k|k-1}+L_k(y_k-\hat x_{k|k-1})\), where \(L_k=\frac{\phi_{k|k-1}}{\phi_{k|k-1}+\sigma_n^2}\) and \(\phi_{k+1|k}=\phi_{k|k}=\frac{\sigma_n^2\phi_{k|k-1}}{\phi_{k|k-1}+\sigma_n^2}\). Writing \(\hat x_k:=\hat x_{k|k}\), \(\phi_k:=\phi_{k|k-1}\) (note \(\phi_k=\phi_{k|k-1}=\phi_{k|k}=\mathbb{E}[(x_k-\hat x_k)^2|I_k]\)):

\[\hat x_0 = \bar x_0+\frac{\phi_0}{\phi_0+\sigma_n^2}(y_0-\bar x_0) = \bar x_0\frac{\sigma_n^2}{\phi_0+\sigma_n^2}+y_0\frac{1}{\phi_0+\sigma_n^2}\]

a weighted average of the prior mean and the first measurement (weights set by the ratio of \(\phi_0\) to \(\sigma_n^2\)). Then \(\hat x_1=\hat x_0+\frac{\phi_1}{\phi_1+\sigma_n^2}(y_1-\hat x_0)\) and \(\hat x_2=\hat x_1+\frac{\phi_2}{\phi_2+\sigma_n^2}(y_2-\hat x_1)\), where \(\phi_1=\frac{\sigma_n^2\phi_0}{\phi_0+\sigma_n^2}\), \(\phi_2=\frac{\sigma_n^2\phi_1}{\phi_1+\sigma_n^2}\); \(\hat x_1\) is a linear combination of \(y_0,y_1\) (and \(\hat x_2\) of \(y_0,y_1,y_2\)).

(ii) \(\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[(x_k-\hat x_k)^2|I_k]=\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_k\). Since \(\phi_k>0\) and \(\phi_{k+1}<\phi_k\) for every \(k\), the limit is zero: as \(k\) grows we accumulate more and more independent measurements of the same constant, so our estimate’s uncertainty is averaged away to nothing.

Problem 6.6 Suppose we wish to estimate the state, at a given time \(k=h\), of a linear system \(x_{k+1}=Ax_k+w_k\), \(k\in\{0,\dots,h-1\}\), with \(A\) invertible so that \(x_k=A^{-1}x_{k+1}-A^{-1}\bar w_{k+1}\), \(\bar w_{k+1}:=w_k\). Output measurements \(y_k=Cx_k+v_k\) are available for \(k\in\{0,\dots,h\}\), and an initial estimate \(\bar x_0\) of \(x_0\) is available (but not the true \(x_0\)).

One way to estimate \(x_h\) (denoted \(\bar x_h\)) is to (i) solve the optimal control problem

\[J_h(x_h) = \min_{\bar w_1,\dots,\bar w_h} (x_0-\bar x_0)^\intercal\bar\Theta_0^{-1}(x_0-\bar x_0)+(y_0-Cx_0)^\intercal V^{-1}(y_0-Cx_0) + \sum_{k=1}^h \bar w_k^\intercal W^{-1}\bar w_k+(y_k-Cx_k)^\intercal V^{-1}(y_k-Cx_k)\]

subject to \(x_k=A^{-1}x_{k+1}-A^{-1}\bar w_{k+1}\), for given positive definite \(W,V,\bar\Theta_0\); then (ii) set \(\bar x_h=\text{argmin } J_h(x_h)\). (Interpretation: estimate \(x_0,\dots,x_h\) by the values best “explained” by small disturbances, output noise, and deviation from the initial guess, then keep the last estimate.)

Show that \(\bar x_h\) coincides with the Kalman filter estimate \(\hat x_{h|h}\) obtained from the usual recursion (with \(\Theta_{0|-1}=\bar\Theta_0\)). [Suggestion: run the DP algorithm forward in time (a “cost-to-come” instead of a cost-to-go), and use the matrix inversion lemma \((A+UCV)^{-1}=A^{-1}-A^{-1}U(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U)^{-1}VA^{-1}\) repeatedly.]

NoteSolution

This is a duality result: the batch/DP estimation problem above is the “dual” of the standard LQR problem, running forward in time instead of backward. The proof shows, by induction on \(k\), that if \(J_k(x_k)=(x_k-\hat x_{k|k})^\intercal\Theta_{k|k}^{-1}(x_k-\hat x_{k|k})+c_k\) for a constant \(c_k\) (true at \(k=0\) by construction, using the matrix inversion lemma to show \(\Theta_{0|0}^{-1}=\bar\Theta_0^{-1}+C^\intercal V^{-1}C\)), then minimizing over \(\bar w_{k+1}\) in the recursive definition of \(J_{k+1}\) – itself a small quadratic minimization solved by completing the square – produces exactly the same form at \(k+1\), with \(\Theta_{k+1|k+1}\) and \(\hat x_{k+1|k+1}\) obeying the standard Kalman filter update (prediction \(\Theta_{k+1|k}=A\Theta_{k|k}A^\intercal+W\) followed by correction with gain \(L_{k+1}=\Theta_{k+1|k}C^\intercal(C\Theta_{k+1|k}C^\intercal+V)^{-1}\)). Carrying the induction to \(k=h\) gives \(J_h(x_h)=(x_h-\hat x_{h|h})^\intercal\Theta_{h|h}^{-1}(x_h-\hat x_{h|h})+c_h\), whose minimizer is trivially \(\bar x_h=\text{argmin }J_h(x_h)=\hat x_{h|h}\), as desired. The full algebraic bookkeeping (several applications of the matrix inversion lemma to massage \(A^{-\intercal}\Theta_{k|k}^{-1}A^{-1}\) terms into the standard \(\Theta_{k+1|k}^{-1}\) form) is in SolutionsPS2.tex if you want to work through every line.

Problem 6.7 Consider the optimal control problem

\[\min \frac{1}{T+1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{k=0}^T x_k^\intercal\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&3\end{bmatrix}x_k + u_k^\intercal\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}u_k\Big]\]

for

\[x_{k+1} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac32&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}x_k+\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}u_k+w_k, \ \ k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\]

where \(x_k,u_k\in\mathbb{R}^2\) and \(w_k\in\mathbb{R}^2\) are iid random vectors.

  1. Find an optimal policy for \(u_0\) and \(u_1\) when \(T=2\).
  2. Compute the expected optimal cost if \(x_0=[1\ \ 2]^\intercal\) and \(\mathbb{E}[w_kw_k^\intercal]=3I\) for every \(k\).
  3. Find the optimal control policy for \(u_k\), \(k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\), when \(T\to\infty\).
NoteSolution

Not reproduced here – taken from the TU/e 2015/2016 sample exam 2, question 3, whose full worked solution is available in the exam archive rather than in SolutionsPS2.tex. This is a direct application of the finite-horizon stochastic LQ result of Lecture 6 (slide “Optimal policy” – gains \(K_k\) from the same Riccati recursion as the deterministic case, with the expected cost picking up the extra \(\text{trace}(P_{k+1}\mathbb{E}[w_kw_k^\intercal])\) terms) for (i)-(ii), and of the averaged-cost infinite-horizon extension (Lecture 6, “Discussion” – cost converging to \(\text{trace}(\bar PW)\)) for (iii); note \(A=\text{diag}(3/2,1)\) is not itself stable, so (iii) is a genuine test of whether \((A,B)\) is controllable enough for the algebraic Riccati equation to still apply.
