Problem 8.1 Consider the switched linear system \(x_{k+1}=A_{\sigma_k}x_k\), \(k\in\mathbb{N}_0\), where \(\sigma_k\in\{0,1\}\) is the control input, and

\[A_0=\begin{bmatrix}0.8&0.1\\0.2&0.8\end{bmatrix},\qquad A_1=\begin{bmatrix}1&0.1\\0&0.5\end{bmatrix}.\]

Suppose that we wish to minimize \(\|x_h\|^2\) for some \(h\in\mathbb{N}\).

  1. Provide the optimal policy for \(\sigma_0\) and \(\sigma_1\) when \(h=2\).
  2. Consider the base policy \(\sigma_k=1\), \(\forall k\in\{0,\dots,h-1\}\). Considering this base policy, derive the rollout policy with horizon \(H=1\) for \(\sigma_0\), \(\sigma_1\), \(\sigma_2\) when \(h=3\).
NoteSolution

(i) This is a standard stage decision problem with \(g_0=g_1=0\) and terminal cost \(g_2(x_2)=x_2^\intercal x_2\). Applying DP backward:

\[J_2(x_2)=x_2^\intercal x_2,\qquad J_1(x_1)=\min_{\sigma_1\in\{0,1\}}x_1^\intercal A_{\sigma_1}^\intercal A_{\sigma_1}x_1 = \min\Big\{x_1^\intercal\begin{bmatrix}0.68&0.24\\0.24&0.65\end{bmatrix}x_1,\ x_1^\intercal\begin{bmatrix}1&0.1\\0.1&0.26\end{bmatrix}x_1\Big\}.\]

The optimal policy at stage \(k=1\) is

\[\sigma_1=\begin{cases}0 & \text{if } x_1^\intercal\begin{bmatrix}0.68&0.24\\0.24&0.65\end{bmatrix}x_1 < x_1^\intercal\begin{bmatrix}1&0.1\\0.1&0.26\end{bmatrix}x_1\\1 & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\]

equivalently \(\sigma_1=0\) if \(x_1^\intercal\begin{bmatrix}-0.32&0.14\\0.14&0.39\end{bmatrix}x_1<0\), a conic partition of the state space (the policy is not unique on the boundary). Writing \(x_1=(y,z)\), the region where \(\sigma_1=0\) is \(\{(y,z):-0.32y^2+0.28zy+0.39z^2<0\}\); setting \(y=\alpha z\) gives \(-0.32\alpha^2+0.28\alpha+0.39=0\), i.e. \(\alpha=1.625\) or \(\alpha=-0.75\), so the boundary consists of the two lines \(y=1.625z\) and \(y=-0.75z\).

At stage \(k=0\),

\[J_1(x_1)=\min\{\|A_0x_1\|^2,\|A_1x_1\|^2\},\qquad J_0(x_0)=\min_{\sigma_0\in\{0,1\}}\{\|A_0A_{\sigma_0}x_0\|^2,\|A_1A_{\sigma_0}x_0\|^2\}=\min\{x_0^\intercal Z_ix_0\}_{i=1}^4,\]

where \(Z_1=(A_0A_0)^\intercal A_0A_0\), \(Z_2=(A_0A_1)^\intercal A_0A_1\), \(Z_3=(A_1A_0)^\intercal A_1A_0\), \(Z_4=(A_1A_1)^\intercal A_1A_1\), and

\[\sigma_0=\begin{cases}0 & \text{if } x_0^\intercal Z_1x_0<x_0^\intercal Z_ix_0 \text{ for } i\in\{2,3,4\}, \text{ or } x_0^\intercal Z_3x_0<x_0^\intercal Z_ix_0\text{ for } i\in\{1,2,4\}\\1 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]

(ii) For the \(H=1\) rollout with base policy \(\sigma_k=1\) for all \(k\), the approximate cost-to-go replaces the exact tail cost by the base policy’s cost from \(H\) steps ahead: for \(h=3\) the tail after one rolled-out decision is \(x_3=A_1A_1A_{\sigma_0}x_0\), so at stage \(0\) the rollout policy minimizes \(\|A_1A_1A_{\sigma_0}x_0\|^2\) over \(\sigma_0\):

\[\sigma_0=\begin{cases}0 & \text{if } \|A_1A_1A_0x_0\|^2<\|A_1A_1A_1x_0\|^2\\1 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]

By the same reasoning, applied one and two steps later,

\[\sigma_1=\begin{cases}0 & \text{if } \|A_1A_0x_1\|^2<\|A_1A_1x_1\|^2\\1 & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad \sigma_2=\begin{cases}0 & \text{if } \|A_0x_2\|^2<\|A_1x_2\|^2\\1 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]

Problem 8.2 Consider the continuous-time switched linear system \(\dot x(t)=B_{\sigma(t)}x(t)\), \(x(t)=\begin{bmatrix}y(t)&z(t)\end{bmatrix}^\intercal\), with

\[B_1=\begin{bmatrix}-(1-\beta)&1\\-9&-(1-\beta)\end{bmatrix},\qquad B_2=\begin{bmatrix}-(1-\beta)&9\\-1&-(1-\beta)\end{bmatrix}.\]

The system is controlled digitally with sampling period \(\tau=0.1\): \(x_{k+1}=A_{\sigma_k}x_k\), \(A_1=e^{B_1\tau}\), \(A_2=e^{B_2\tau}\).

  1. Suppose \(\beta=1\). (a) Provide a policy for \(\sigma(t)\) stabilizing the continuous-time system. (b) Provide a policy for \(\sigma_k\) stabilizing the discrete-time system.
  2. Consider the cost \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty x_k^\intercal x_k\). (a) Derive the policy obtained by MPC with horizon \(H=2\) (minimizing \(x_k^\intercal x_k+x_{k+1}^\intercal x_{k+1}\) at each \(k\)), and describe the switching regions for \(\beta=1\) and \(\beta=0.9\). Does this policy stabilize the discrete-time system? (b) For \(\beta=0.9\), derive the rollout policy with horizon \(H=1\) using base policy \(\sigma_k=1\) for every \(k\ge0\). Is this base policy usable when \(\beta=1\)?
NoteSolution

(i)(a) With \(\beta=1\), \(B_1=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-9&0\end{bmatrix}\) and \(B_2=\begin{bmatrix}0&9\\-1&0\end{bmatrix}\): mode 1 gives \(\dot y=z\), \(\dot z=-9y\), and mode 2 gives \(\dot y=9z\), \(\dot z=-y\) – both undamped oscillators (eigenvalues \(\pm3i\)), individually marginal rather than stabilizing. Using \(V(x)=y^2+z^2\) as a candidate Lyapunov function, \(\dot V=2y\dot y+2z\dot z\) evaluates to \(\dot V=-16yz\) under mode 1 and \(\dot V=16yz\) under mode 2. Switching to whichever mode makes \(\dot V\) negative therefore stabilizes the system:

\[\sigma(t)=\begin{cases}1 & \text{if } y(t)z(t)\ge0\\2 & \text{if } y(t)z(t)<0.\end{cases}\]

(The source figure for this problem could not be reproduced here; this policy is verified directly by the sign of \(\dot V\) above rather than read off a plot.)

(i)(b) Since \(\tau=0.1\) is small compared with the oscillation period of either mode (\(2\pi/3\approx2.1\)), the same state-based rule stabilizes the sampled-data system: \(\sigma_k=1\) if \(y_kz_k\ge0\), \(\sigma_k=2\) if \(y_kz_k<0\).

(ii)(a) At time \(k\), \(x_k\) is fixed, so minimizing \(x_k^\intercal x_k+x_{k+1}^\intercal x_{k+1}=x_k^\intercal x_k+x_k^\intercal A_{\sigma_k}^\intercal A_{\sigma_k}x_k\) over \(\sigma_k\) is equivalent to minimizing \(x_k^\intercal A_{\sigma_k}^\intercal A_{\sigma_k}x_k\) alone:

\[\sigma_k=\begin{cases}1 & \text{if } x_k^\intercal(A_1^\intercal A_1-A_2^\intercal A_2)x_k<0\\2 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]

Computing \(A_1,A_2\) numerically: for \(\beta=1\), \(A_1^\intercal A_1-A_2^\intercal A_2=\begin{bmatrix}0.776&-1.506\\-1.506&-0.776\end{bmatrix}\), an indefinite matrix whose eigenvalues are \(\pm1.69\); the switching region boundary is the pair of lines \(y=4.12z\) and \(y=-0.243z\) through the origin. For \(\beta=0.9\) the same computation gives \(A_1^\intercal A_1-A_2^\intercal A_2=\begin{bmatrix}0.761&-1.476\\-1.476&-0.761\end{bmatrix}\), with essentially the same boundary lines. What differs between the two cases is the open-loop behaviour of each mode alone: at \(\beta=1\), \(|{\rm eig}(A_1)|=|{\rm eig}(A_2)|=1\) (marginally stable/undamped, matching (i)); at \(\beta=0.9\), \(|{\rm eig}(A_1)|=|{\rm eig}(A_2)|=0.99<1\) (each mode is already, if barely, asymptotically stable on its own).

Consequently, at \(\beta=1\) the one-step-lookahead MPC policy is exactly the greedy, myopic version of the stabilizing switching rule from (i)(b) – since \(H=2\) only looks one step ahead, it need not accumulate the sustained sign pattern in \(y_kz_k\) that true stabilization requires, so MPC does not guarantee stability of the switched system in general (this is the standard caveat that MPC’s finite lookahead is not automatically stabilizing). At \(\beta=0.9\), stability holds regardless of which mode MPC selects at each step, since both individual modes already contract; the switching decision only affects the transient, not asymptotic stability.

(ii)(b) For \(\beta=0.9\), \(A_1\) is stable (\(|{\rm eig}(A_1)|=0.99<1\)), so the infinite-horizon cost of the base policy \(\sigma_k=1\) for all \(k\) is \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty x_k^\intercal x_k=x_0^\intercal Px_0\), where \(P\) solves the Lyapunov equation \(A_1^\intercal PA_1-P=-I\) (the general result proved in Unit 5, Problem 5.4). Numerically, \(P=\begin{bmatrix}250.3&-2.15\\-2.15&28.3\end{bmatrix}\) (the large entries reflect that \(A_1\) is close to the stability boundary). The \(H=1\) rollout policy is then

\[\sigma_k=\begin{cases}1 & \text{if } x_k^\intercal(A_1^\intercal PA_1-A_2^\intercal PA_2)x_k<0\\2 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]

This base policy cannot be used when \(\beta=1\): there, \(A_1\) is only marginally stable (\(|{\rm eig}(A_1)|=1\)), so the Lyapunov equation \(A_1^\intercal PA_1-P=-I\) has no positive definite solution, and the cost of applying \(\sigma_k=1\) forever is unbounded – the base policy is not stabilizing and so cannot anchor a rollout.

Problem 8.3 Consider the system \(x_{k+1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix}x_k+\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}u_k\), \(u_k\in\mathbb{R}\), \(x_k\in\mathbb{R}^2\), with two available stabilizing controllers \(u_k=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}&-1\end{bmatrix}x_k\) and \(u_k=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{3}&-\frac{6}{5}\end{bmatrix}x_k\). The goal is to switch between the two to minimize \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty\|x_k\|^2\).

  1. Formulate the problem as optimal control of a switched linear system \(x_{k+1}=A_{\sigma_k}x_k\), \(\sigma_k\in\{1,2\}\), with cost \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty x_k^\intercal Qx_k\) for some positive definite \(Q\). Provide \(A_1\), \(A_2\), \(Q\).
  2. Using the Lyapunov equation, show that the cost of the base policy \(\sigma_k=1\), \(\forall k\), takes the form \(x_0^\intercal\bar Px_0\). Provide \(\bar P\).
  3. Considering the same base policy, derive the rollout policy with horizon \(H=1\) for \(\{\sigma_k\}_{k=0}^\infty\).
  4. Let \(\hat J(x_0)\) denote the cost when the \(H=1\) rollout policy is applied for a given \(x_0\). It can be shown that \(\hat J(x_0)\le x_0^\intercal\bar Px_0\) for every \(x_0\). Use this to establish that the rollout policy asymptotically stabilizes the system.
NoteSolution

(i) Substituting each controller into the dynamics, \(A_1=A+BK_1=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\-\frac{1}{2}&0\end{bmatrix}\) and \(A_2=A+BK_2=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\-\frac{1}{3}&-\frac{1}{5}\end{bmatrix}\), and since the cost is \(\sum\|x_k\|^2=\sum x_k^\intercal x_k\), \(Q=I\).

(ii) \(A_1\) is asymptotically stable (\({\rm eig}(A_1)=0.5\pm0.5i\), \(|{\rm eig}(A_1)|=1/\sqrt2\)), so by the Lyapunov-equation result of Unit 5, Problem 5.4, the cost of applying \(\sigma_k=1\) forever is \(x_0^\intercal\bar Px_0\) where \(\bar P\) solves \(A_1^\intercal\bar PA_1-\bar P=-Q\). Solving exactly gives

\[\bar P=\begin{bmatrix}3&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}.\]

(iii) The \(H=1\) rollout policy at state \(x_k\) minimizes \(x_k^\intercal Qx_k+x_{k+1}^\intercal\bar Px_{k+1}=x_k^\intercal Qx_k+x_k^\intercal A_{\sigma_k}^\intercal\bar PA_{\sigma_k}x_k\) over \(\sigma_k\in\{1,2\}\); since the \(Q\)-term does not depend on \(\sigma_k\), this is equivalent to minimizing \(x_k^\intercal A_{\sigma_k}^\intercal\bar PA_{\sigma_k}x_k\). Computing exactly, \(A_1^\intercal\bar PA_1=\begin{bmatrix}2&2\\2&3\end{bmatrix}\) and \(A_2^\intercal\bar PA_2=\begin{bmatrix}19/9&11/5\\11/5&59/25\end{bmatrix}\), so

\[A_1^\intercal\bar PA_1-A_2^\intercal\bar PA_2=\begin{bmatrix}-1/9&-1/5\\-1/5&16/25\end{bmatrix},\]

and, writing \(x_k=(y,z)\), the (stationary, since the base-policy cost-to-go \(\bar P\) is the same at every stage) rollout policy is

\[\sigma_k=\begin{cases}1 & \text{if } -\frac{1}{9}y^2-\frac{2}{5}yz+\frac{16}{25}z^2<0\\2 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]

(iv) (This part is not covered by the source solutions; the argument below is derived independently.) Because the rollout policy is stationary and state-feedback, applying it from any state \(x\) gives a well-defined cost \(\hat J(x)=\sum_{j\ge0}x_j^\intercal Qx_j\) (trajectory started at \(x_0=x\)), which decomposes recursively as \(\hat J(x_k)=x_k^\intercal Qx_k+\hat J(x_{k+1})\) along the closed-loop trajectory generated by the policy. Combined with the given bound \(\hat J(x)\le x^\intercal\bar Px\) (valid for every state, in particular for every \(x_k\) visited along the trajectory), \(\hat J\) is a valid candidate Lyapunov function for the closed-loop system: it is positive definite (\(\hat J(x)\ge0\) since \(Q\ge0\), with equality only at \(x=0\)), finite everywhere (bounded above by \(x^\intercal\bar Px<\infty\)), and satisfies

\[\hat J(x_k)-\hat J(x_{k+1})=x_k^\intercal Qx_k\ge0,\]

with strict inequality whenever \(x_k\neq0\) (since \(Q=I>0\)). So \(\hat J(x_k)\) is non-increasing along the trajectory and strictly decreasing unless \(x_k=0\); being bounded below by \(0\), it converges, which forces the increments \(x_k^\intercal Qx_k\to0\), and since \(Q>0\) this means \(\|x_k\|\to0\). Hence the \(H=1\) rollout policy asymptotically stabilizes the system.

Problem 8.4 Consider \(\begin{bmatrix}\dot y(t)\\\dot z(t)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\theta(t))\\\sin(\theta(t))\end{bmatrix}\), where \(\theta(t)\in[-\pi,\pi)\) is the control input and \(x(t)=\begin{bmatrix}y(t)&z(t)\end{bmatrix}^\intercal\) is the state. The input is implemented digitally, \(\theta(t)=\theta_k\) for \(t\in[k\tau,(k+1)\tau)\), giving the Euler discretization \(x_{k+1}=x_k+\tau\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\theta_k)\\\sin(\theta_k)\end{bmatrix}\). The goal is to minimize \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty\|x_k\|_Q^2\) for positive definite \(Q\).

  1. Show that the MPC policy with horizon \(H=2\) is \(\theta_k(x_k)=\mu(x_k)\), where

\[\mu(x)=\begin{cases}\arctan\big(\tfrac{v_2(x)}{v_1(x)}\big) & \text{if } v_1(x)\neq0\\\frac{\pi}{2} & \text{if } v_2(x)\ge0,\ v_1(x)=0\\-\frac{\pi}{2} & \text{if } v_2(x)<0,\ v_1(x)=0,\end{cases}\]

and \(v(x)=(v_1(x),v_2(x))\) solves \(\min_{v\in\mathbb{R}^2}\|x+\tau v\|_Q^2\) s.t. \(\|v\|^2=1\). (ii) Provide the optimal control input at stage \(0\) when \(\tau=0.1\), \(Q=I\), \(x_0=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\end{bmatrix}^\intercal\).

NoteSolution

(i) At time \(\ell\), the \(H=2\) MPC problem is \(\min_{\theta_\ell}\|x_\ell\|_Q^2+\|x_{\ell+1}\|_Q^2\); since \(x_\ell\) is fixed, this is equivalent to \(\min_{\theta_\ell}\|x_\ell+\tau[\cos\theta_\ell,\sin\theta_\ell]^\intercal\|_Q^2\). Because \(\{(\cos\theta,\sin\theta):\theta\in[-\pi,\pi)\}=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^2:\|v\|^2=1\}\), this is equivalent to the constrained problem \(\min_{v}\|x_\ell+\tau v\|_Q^2\) s.t. \(\|v\|^2=1\), with \(\cos\theta=v_1\), \(\sin\theta=v_2\) recovering \(\theta\) via the four-quadrant relation stated (using \(\arctan\) on the ratio when \(v_1\neq0\), and the two vertical-axis cases otherwise).

(ii) With \(\tau=0.1\), \(Q=I\), \(x_0=[1\ \ 1]^\intercal\), the problem is \(\min_{v_1,v_2}(1+0.1v_1)^2+(1+0.1v_2)^2\) s.t. \(v_1^2+v_2^2=1\). The Lagrangian \(L=(1+0.1v_1)^2+(1+0.1v_2)^2+\lambda(v_1^2+v_2^2-1)\) gives, from \(\partial L/\partial v_1=\partial L/\partial v_2=0\), \(v_1=v_2=\frac{-1}{\lambda+0.1}\); substituting into the constraint gives \(\lambda=-0.1\pm\sqrt2\). The two stationary points are

\[(v_1,v_2)=\Big(-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2},-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big)\ \Rightarrow\ \theta=\tfrac{5\pi}{4}\ (\text{minimizer}),\qquad (v_1,v_2)=\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2},\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big)\ \Rightarrow\ \theta=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\ (\text{maximizer}).\]

The optimal control input at stage \(0\) is therefore \(\theta_0=5\pi/4\) (pointing away from the origin’s opposite side, i.e. steering back toward the origin from \(x_0=[1\ \ 1]^\intercal\)).
