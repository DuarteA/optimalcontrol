1 Core exercises

Problem 5.1 Consider a first order discrete-time linear system

\[x_{k+1} = \alpha x_k+u_k, \ \ k\in \mathbb{N}_0,\]

and the cost

\[\sum_{k=0}^q x_k^2+\gamma u_k^2.\]

  1. Considering \(\alpha=1\), \(\gamma=1\) and \(q=2\), determine the policy for \(u_0\), \(u_1\), and \(u_2\) that minimizes the cost.
  2. Considering \(\alpha=1\), \(\gamma=1\) and \(q=\infty\), determine the policy for \(\{u_k, k\in\mathbb{N}_0\}\) that minimizes the cost.
  3. Suppose now that \(|\alpha|<1\) and \(q=\infty\). Provide and interpret the policy that minimizes the cost when \(\gamma\to\infty\).
NoteSolution

(a) For \(\alpha=1\), \(\gamma=1\), \(q=2\), we have \(x_{k+1}=x_k+u_k\) and \(\sum_{k=0}^2 \underbrace{x_k^2+u_k^2}_{g(x_k,u_k)} + \underbrace{0}_{J_3(x_3)}\), a standard stage decision problem. Applying DP:

Step 1. \(J_2(x_2) = \min_{u_2} x_2^2+u_2^2 = x_2^2\), where \(u_2=\mu(x_2)=0\ \forall x_2\).

Step 2. \(J_1(x_1) = \min_{u_1} x_1^2+u_1^2+(x_1+u_1)^2 = \min_{u_1} 2x_1^2+2u_1^2+2x_1u_1\). Differentiating and equating to zero: \(4u_1+2x_1=0\), so \(u_1=-\frac{1}{2}x_1\) and \(J_1(x_1)=\frac{3}{2}x_1^2\).

Step 3. \(J_0(x_0) = \min_{u_0} x_0^2+u_0^2+\frac{3}{2}(x_0+u_0)^2 = \min_{u_0} \frac{5}{2}x_0^2+\frac{5}{2}u_0^2+3x_0u_0\). Differentiating and equating to zero: \(5u_0+3x_0=0\), so \(u_0=-\frac{3}{5}x_0\) and \(J_0(x_0)=1.6x_0^2\).

Alternatively, iterate the (scalar) Riccati equation \(p_k=p_{k+1}+1-\frac{p_{k+1}^2}{1+p_{k+1}}\) for \(k\in\{2,1,0\}\), \(p_3=0\), with gains \(K_k=-\frac{p_{k+1}}{1+p_{k+1}}\) (costs-to-go \(x_k^2p_k\)). This gives \(p_2=1\), \(K_2=0\); \(p_1=\frac{3}{2}\), \(K_1=-\frac{1}{2}\); \(p_0=1.6\), \(K_0=-\frac{3}{5}\) – matching the by-hand recursion.

(b) Since the model is controllable and the cost matrix is positive definite, the backward iteration converges to a limit \(p\) satisfying the algebraic Riccati equation \(p=p+1-\frac{p^2}{1+p}\), i.e. \(p^2-p-1=0\), with roots \(p=\frac{1\pm\sqrt5}{2}\). Taking the positive-definite root, \(p=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}=1.618\), so the optimal cost is \(x_0^2p\) and the optimal policy is \(u_k=Kx_k\), \(k\in\mathbb{N}_0\), where \(K=-\frac{p}{1+p}=-\frac{1+\sqrt5}{3+\sqrt5}=-0.618\).

(c) \(P(\gamma)\) satisfies \(p=\alpha^2p+1-p^2\alpha^2/(\gamma+p)\) and converges to \(\frac{1}{1-\alpha^2}\) as \(\gamma\to\infty\). Thus \(K(\gamma)=-\frac{\alpha^2P(\gamma)^2}{\gamma+P(\gamma)}\) converges to zero, so the optimal policy is \(u_k=0\) for every \(k\). Interpretation: if control is expensive and the system is already stable (\(\alpha<1\)), it is optimal to just let it run open loop. The resulting cost is obtained from iterating \(x_{k+1}=\alpha x_k\): \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty x_k^2 = \sum_{k=0}^\infty (\alpha^2)^kx_0^2 = \frac{1}{1-\alpha^2}x_0^2\).

Problem 5.2 Consider the standard linear quadratic problem

\[\min \sum_{k=0}^{h-1} x_k^\intercal Qx_k+u_k^\intercal Ru_k+2x_k^\intercal Su_k+g_h(x_h) \qquad x_{k+1}=Ax_k+Bu_k\]

for

\[Q=\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&3\end{bmatrix} \quad S=\begin{bmatrix}0.1&0\\0&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix} \quad R=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix} \quad A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0\\-3&4&1\\0&0&3\end{bmatrix} \quad B=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}\]

and \(g_h(x_h)=x_h^\intercal Q_hx_h\), \(Q_h=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&2&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}\).

  1. Provide the gains \(K_k\) of the optimal policy \(u_k=K_kx_k\), \(k\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), when \(h=5\), by iterating the Riccati equations.
  2. Using the same recursion, obtain the optimal policy \(u_k=Kx_k\) when \(g_h(x_h)=0\) and \(h=\infty\).
  3. Confirm the results of (ii) using the algebraic Riccati equation directly (e.g. via dlqr.m in Matlab, or scipy.linalg.solve_discrete_are in Python).
NoteSolution

(i) Iterating the Riccati/gain equations backward from \(P_5=Q_5=Q_h\) gives

\[K_0=\begin{bmatrix}2.8560&-3.3810&-0.9790\\0.0263&0.0605&-1.4179\end{bmatrix} \qquad K_1=\begin{bmatrix}2.8494&-3.3649&-0.9777\\0.0271&0.0589&-1.4180\end{bmatrix} \qquad K_2=\begin{bmatrix}2.8191&-3.2769&-0.9749\\0.0344&0.0450&-1.4161\end{bmatrix}\]

\[K_3=\begin{bmatrix}2.7408&-3.0849&-0.9967\\0.0829&-0.0448&-1.3211\end{bmatrix} \qquad K_4=\begin{bmatrix}1.9667&-2.6667&-0.6667\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}\]

The optimal policy is \(u_k=K_kx_k\), \(k\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\).

(ii) Iterating with a large horizon (e.g. \(h=20\)) the gains converge, \(\bar K=K_0=\begin{bmatrix}2.8570&-3.3830&-0.9792\\0.0262&0.0607&-1.4179\end{bmatrix}\). The optimal policy is \(u_k=\bar Kx_k\).

(iii) Solving the algebraic Riccati equation directly reproduces the same gains as in (ii).

2 Additional exercises

Problem 5.3 A schematic model of a DC motor is depicted below. The torque generated by the motor is assumed proportional to the armature current, \(T=K_ti\), and the back emf \(e\) is assumed proportional to the angular velocity of the shaft, \(e=K_e\dot\theta\). Newton’s law and Kirchhoff’s voltage law give

\[J\ddot\theta+b\dot\theta = K_ti \qquad L\frac{di}{dt}+Ri = V-K_e\dot\theta\]

Assuming the inductance is very small, \(L\approx 0\), this reduces to \(J\ddot\theta+\big(b+\frac{K_tK_e}{R}\big)\dot\theta = \frac{K_t}{R}V\). Parameters: \(J=0.01\), \(b=0.1\), \(K_e=0.01\), \(K_t=0.01\), \(R=1\), \(L=0\).

  1. Find a state-space representation for \(x(t) := [\theta(t)\ \ \dot\theta(t)]^\intercal\).
  2. Discretize this model for \(\tau=0.1\), giving \(x_{k+1}=A_dx_k+B_du_k\) with \(x_k:=x(\tau k)\).
  3. Suppose we want to regulate the state to zero while minimizing \(J=\sum_{k=0}^\infty x_k^\intercal Qx_k+u_k^\intercal Ru_k\), \(Q=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\0&5\end{bmatrix}\), \(R=0.1\).    (a) Compute the LQR gain \(K^*\) (\(u_k=K^*x_k\)) and the cost matrix \(P^*\) (\(x_0^\intercal P^*x_0\)).    (b) Compute the control that regulates the state to zero in the minimum number of steps (deadbeat, cf. Problem 5.5), as a function of \(x_0\); show the resulting cost is \(x_0^\intercal P_fx_0\) for some \(P_f\).    (c) Compare the costs obtained in (a) and (b).
NoteSolution

(i) \(\dot x = \begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&-\alpha\end{bmatrix}x+\begin{bmatrix}0\\\beta\end{bmatrix}V\), where \(\alpha=\frac{b+K_tK_e/R}{J}=10.01\) and \(\beta=\frac{K_t}{RJ}=1\).

(ii) With \(A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&-\alpha\end{bmatrix}\), \(B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\\beta\end{bmatrix}\): \(A_d=e^{A\tau}=\begin{bmatrix}1.0000&0.0632\\0&0.3675\end{bmatrix}\), \(B_d=\int_0^\tau e^{As}ds\,B = \begin{bmatrix}0.0037\\0.0632\end{bmatrix}\).

(iii)(a) \(K = -\begin{bmatrix}3.9698&1.3993\end{bmatrix}\), \(P=\begin{bmatrix}57.4811&4.6278\\4.6278&6.0073\end{bmatrix}\).

(iii)(b) From the controllability-matrix formula of Problem 5.5, \(\begin{bmatrix}K_0\\K_1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A_dB_d & B_d\end{bmatrix}^{-1}(-A_d^2)\), which leads to \(x_1=(A_d+B_dK_0)x_0\) and \(x_2=0\). The cost is \(x_0^\intercal Qx_0+x_0^\intercal K_0^\intercal RK_0x_0+x_0^\intercal(A_d+B_dK_0)^\intercal Q(A_d+B_dK_0)x_0+x_0^\intercal K_1^\intercal RK_1x_0 = x_0^\intercal P_fx_0\), where \(P_f=Q+K_0^\intercal RK_0+(A_d+B_dK_0)^\intercal Q(A_d+B_dK_0)+K_1^\intercal RK_1\). Numerically, \(K_0=\begin{bmatrix}-158.2638&-12.4309\end{bmatrix}\), \(K_1=\begin{bmatrix}58.1638&2.4309\end{bmatrix}\), \(P_f=\begin{bmatrix}3345.4&231.78\\231.78&21.92\end{bmatrix}\).

(iii)(c) \(x_0^\intercal P^*x_0 \leq x_0^\intercal P_fx_0\) for every \(x_0\), since \(P_f-P^*\) is positive definite – driving the state to zero as fast as possible (deadbeat) is never cheaper than the LQR-optimal trade-off.

Problem 5.4 In this problem we wish to prove that the cost \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty x_k^\intercal Qx_k\), \(Q=Q^\intercal\), for an asymptotically stable discrete-time system \(x_{k+1}=Ax_k\), is given by \(x_0^\intercal Px_0\), where \(P\) solves the Lyapunov equation \(A^\intercal PA-P=-Q\).

  1. Prove that \(\sum_{k=0}^T a^k=\frac{1-a^{T+1}}{1-a}\) for \(a\in(-1,1)\), by multiplying the left-hand side by \(\frac{1-a}{1-a}\).
  2. Use (a) to prove the desired result for \(A\in\mathbb{R}\), \(|A|<1\).
  3. Generalize (a)-(b) to \(A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\) with eigenvalues inside the unit circle.
  4. Confirm that if \(A\) and \(Q\) are replaced by \((A+BK)\) and \(Q+K^\intercal RK\), for \(K=-(B^\intercal PB+R)^{-1}B^\intercal PA\), the Lyapunov equation coincides with the Riccati equation \(P=A^\intercal PA+Q-A^\intercal PB(B^\intercal PB+R)^{-1}B^\intercal PA\). Explain why.
NoteSolution

(a) \(\sum_{k=0}^T a^k = \frac{1-a}{1-a}\sum_{k=0}^T a^k = \frac{1}{1-a}\sum_{k=0}^T(a^k-a^{k+1}) = \frac{1}{1-a}(1-a^{T+1})\) (telescoping sum).

(b) \(J=\sum_{k=0}^T x_k^\intercal Qx_k = x_0^\intercal\big[\sum_{k=0}^T (A^k)^\intercal QA^k\big]x_0\). For scalar \(A\), this is \(x_0^2Q\sum_{k=0}^T(A^2)^k\). As \(T\to\infty\), using (a) with \(a=A^2\), \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty(A^2)^k=\frac{1}{1-A^2}\), so \(J=x_0^2Q\frac{1}{1-A^2}\). The Lyapunov equation \(A^\intercal PA-P=-Q\) gives \(Q=P(1-A^2)\), hence \(J=x_0^2P\).

(c) \(J=x_0^\intercal\big[\sum_{k=0}^T(A^k)^\intercal QA^k\big]x_0\); substituting \(Q=P-A^\intercal PA\) (from the Lyapunov equation) telescopes the sum so that only the \(k=0\) and \(k=T+1\) terms survive: \(J=x_0^\intercal\big[P-(A^{T+1})^\intercal PA^{T+1}\big]x_0\), and the last term vanishes as \(T\to\infty\) since \(A\) is asymptotically stable.

(d) Direct substitution. If we apply the LQR policy \(u_k=Kx_k\) to \(\min\sum_{k=0}^\infty x_k^\intercal Qx_k+u_k^\intercal Ru_k\) for \(x_{k+1}=Ax_k+Bu_k\), we get the cost \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty x_k^\intercal\underbrace{(Q+K^\intercal RK)}_{\bar Q}x_k\) for the closed loop \(x_{k+1}=\underbrace{(A+BK)}_{\bar A}x_k\) – i.e. the Riccati equation is exactly the Lyapunov equation for the closed-loop cost, confirming (via (a)-(c)) that \(x_0^\intercal Px_0\) is indeed the LQR optimal cost.

Problem 5.5 Consider the discrete-time linear time-invariant system \(x_{k+1}=Ax_k+Bu_k\), \(x_k\in\mathbb{R}^n\), \(u_k\in\mathbb{R}\), \(k\in\mathbb{N}_0\), with arbitrary initial condition \(x_0\). Suppose we wish to drive the state to a desired final value \(\bar x\). Show that the control values

\[\begin{bmatrix}u_0\\u_1\\\vdots\\u_{n-1}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}A^{n-1}B & A^{n-2}B & \cdots & B\end{bmatrix}^{-1}(\bar x-A^nx_0)\]

result in \(x_n=\bar x\), provided the matrix on the right-hand side is invertible (in which case the system is said to be controllable).

NoteSolution

From \(x_{k+1}=Ax_k+Bu_k\) we conclude that \(x_n = A^nx_0+\begin{bmatrix}A^{n-1}B & A^{n-2}B & \cdots & B\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u_0\\u_1\\\vdots\\u_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}\). Setting \(x_n=\bar x\) and solving for the control vector (assuming invertibility) gives exactly the stated formula.

Problem 5.6 The goal of this exercise is to provide examples of linear quadratic regulation problems \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty x_k^\intercal Qx_k+u_k^\intercal Ru_k\), \(x_{k+1}=Ax_k+Bu_k\), for which the optimal control policy cannot be obtained by solving the algebraic Riccati equation \(P=A^\intercal PA+Q-(A^\intercal PB)(B^\intercal PB+R)^{-1}(B^\intercal PA)\).

  1. Provide an example of a pair \((A,B)\) for which there does not exist a positive definite solution to the algebraic Riccati equation for any \(Q\) and \(R\).
  2. Provide an example of a pair \((A,Q)\) for which there does not exist a positive definite solution for any \(B\) and \(R\).
NoteSolution

(i) Take \(B=0\) and \(A\) not Hurwitz (e.g. \(A=2\), a scalar). The model reduces to \(x_{k+1}=Ax_k\), whose trajectories blow up for any nonzero initial condition (\((A,B)\) is not controllable/stabilizable). The algebraic Riccati equation becomes \(P=4P+Q\), whose unique solution \(P=-Q/3\) is not positive definite for any positive semi-definite \(Q\).

(ii) Take \(Q=0\), \(A=0\): the algebraic Riccati equation becomes \(P=0\), whose solution is not positive definite (the pair \((A,N)\) with \(Q=NN^\intercal\) fails observability/detectability).

Problem 5.7 The goal of this exercise is to show that we do not need \(R\) to be positive definite to obtain the optimal policy for the standard linear regulator problem with the algebraic Riccati equation. Consider the setting of Problem 5.2 with

\[A=\begin{bmatrix}1/2&1\\1&3\end{bmatrix} \qquad B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix} \qquad R=0 \qquad S=0 \qquad Q=I \qquad Q_h=I\]

  1. Provide the gains \(K_k\) of the optimal policy \(u_k=K_kx_k\), \(k\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), when \(h=5\), by iterating the Riccati equations.
  2. Using the same recursion, obtain the optimal policy \(u_k=Kx_k\) for \(h=\infty\).
  3. Try to solve the algebraic Riccati equation directly with a solver that requires \(R>0\) (e.g. dlqr.m) and see that it does not work.
NoteSolution

(i) Iterating the Riccati recursion with these parameters gives

\[K_0=\begin{bmatrix}-1.1328&-3.2656\end{bmatrix} \quad K_1=\begin{bmatrix}-1.1328&-3.2655\end{bmatrix} \quad K_2=\begin{bmatrix}-1.1324&-3.2647\end{bmatrix} \quad K_3=\begin{bmatrix}-1.1250&-3.2500\end{bmatrix} \quad K_4=\begin{bmatrix}-1&-3\end{bmatrix}\]

The optimal policy is \(u_k=K_kx_k\), \(k\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\).

(ii) For a large horizon (e.g. \(h=20\)) the gains converge to \(\bar K=K_0=\begin{bmatrix}-1.1328&-3.2656\end{bmatrix}\). The optimal policy is \(u_k=\bar Kx_k\).

(iii) A solver requiring \(R\) positive definite (like dlqr.m) fails here, but (ii) shows this is not actually needed for the algebraic Riccati equation itself to admit a solution and define the optimal policy – the requirement \(R>0\) is a sufficient condition used by some solvers/proofs, not a necessary one.
