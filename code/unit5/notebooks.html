1 Finite-horizon LQR for a double integrator

The worked example of the lecture: the finite-horizon cost \(\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}x_k^\intercal Qx_k+u_k^\intercal Ru_k+x_h^\intercal Q_hx_h\) applied to a double integrator. Every parameter is exposed, so it is the one to open first if you want to feel what \(Q\), \(R\), \(Q_h\) and the horizon \(h\) actually do to the closed loop – raise \(R\) and watch the input get lazier, raise \(Q_h\) and watch the terminal state get pulled in harder.

Download A_5_LQfinhorizondoubleintegrator.ipynb

2 Riccati equations and the infinite-horizon limit

The same double integrator, but with the horizon pushed out: the notebook iterates the backward Riccati recursion and shows \(P_k\to\bar P\) and \(K_k\to\bar K\), so the solution of the algebraic Riccati equation appears as the limit of the finite-horizon one. This is the computational counterpart of the convergence argument in the notes.

Download A_5_Riccatiequations.ipynb

3 Reference tracking with LQR

Stabilization drives the state to zero; tracking asks it to follow a reference \(r_k\). Minimizing \(\sum_k\|Mx_k-r_k\|^2+u_k^\intercal Ru_k\) by dynamic programming gives an optimal policy of the affine form \(u_k=K_kx_k+L_k\), where – and this is the point worth taking away – the gains \(K_k\) are exactly those of the stabilization problem, and all the reference information is carried by the feedforward term \(L_k\).

Download A_5_N_referencetrackinglqr.ipynb

4 LQR for the inverted pendulum

The infinite-horizon regulator applied to the linearized inverted-pendulum-on-a-cart, under full state feedback – the same plant that returns in Unit 6 once the state has to be estimated rather than measured. A good reference point for how much the LQG controller of the next unit gives up when only a noisy cart position is available.

Download A_5_K_LQRinvertedpendulum.ipynb

5 Positioning a flexible head-disk drive

A genuinely industrial example: positioning the read/write head of a disk drive, where the arm is flexible, so the torque applied at the base and the angle of the head are separated by a lightly damped resonance. The model is the two-inertia system of Franklin, Powell and Emami-Naeini, and it shows what LQR does with a plant whose dynamics actively fight a fast response.

Download A_5_K_PositioningHeadDisk.ipynb

6 Tracking an ellipsoidal trajectory under disturbances

A particle with first-order velocity dynamics in the plane is asked to follow an ellipsoidal reference while random disturbances push it off course, and the cost penalizes the variance of the tracking error. It combines the reference-tracking formulation above with the stochastic setting that opens Unit 6.

Download A_5_K_Tracking_lqr.ipynb

7 Smooth trajectory generation with LQR

The least obvious use of the machinery in this unit. Given \(N\) waypoints in 3D and the times at which they must be met, the task is to produce a trajectory through them that is smooth up to a given derivative order. Posing this as an LQR problem – the control being the highest derivative, the cost penalizing deviation from a piecewise-linear interpolant – turns a trajectory-design question into exactly the Riccati recursion of this unit. Worth reading as evidence that “linear quadratic” is a much wider hammer than “drive the state to zero”.
