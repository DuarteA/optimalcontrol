Four notebooks, one per section of the lecture. Each imports the matching module from this folder rather than repeating its code, so the notebooks and the slides are running exactly the same solvers.

Start with the slides if you have not seen them.

1 1. Direct shooting

A_9_ShootingPendulum.ipynb – single and multiple shooting on the minimum-effort pendulum swing-up, built on shooting.py.

Eliminating the states by forward simulation, the conditioning of the sensitivity Jacobian as the horizon grows, and why cutting the horizon into segments repairs it. The notebook closes on the fact that decides most practical outcomes: single shooting and multiple shooting return different solutions here, and warm-starting single shooting moves it onto the better one. The initial guess, not the method, picks the local optimum.

2 2. Direct collocation

A_9_CollocationPendulum.ipynb – trapezoidal and Hermite-Simpson collocation, built on collocation.py.

States and controls at every node, dynamics as defect constraints. Then the question the reported numbers do not answer: is the answer a trajectory the pendulum would actually follow? Simulating the control open-loop shows that at the default penalty weight it is not, and that the attractive-looking cost was bought by letting the dynamics drift. Raising the weight fixes it, and all five methods of the unit are then scored on the same footing.

3 3. Iterative LQR

A_9_ILQRPendulum.ipynb – iLQR, built on ilqr.py.

The backward Riccati-like pass and the forward line search, its near-quadratic convergence, and the local optimum it finds from a zero-control start – which, as the notebook shows, single shooting finds too. Ends with the bridges back to Unit 5 and Unit 8 and forward to Unit 10.

4 4. The two case studies

A_9_EVCaseStudies.ipynb – energy-optimal and time-optimal driving for an electric vehicle, built on vehicle.py.

The two problems that close the lecture, and the only ones here that cannot be done by hand. Energy-optimal: why the hill costs anything at all, the 7% saving over cruise control, and the fact that the optimal trajectory never brakes. Time-optimal: a free horizon, a bang-bang solution, a near-exact reference from Pontryagin’s minimum principle, and the fourth-order scheme that gains nothing because the solution is not smooth.

5 Running them

The three pendulum notebooks need only numpy and matplotlib. The case studies additionally need scipy – see README.md for why that one module is the exception.

All four are stored without output, so the first run computes everything from scratch. The longest cells are the grid-refinement sweeps, a couple of minutes each on a laptop.
